## Blockers

1. **`when_rome_fell` card block 1 (both locales):** "deposed Romulus Augustulus, a boy ruling in his father's name" / "um menino que reinava em nome do pai" has it the wrong way round. Britannica (Romulus Augustulus), cited for c3, says "For about 12 months Orestes ruled Italy in his son's name". Claim c3 has it right ("put on the throne by his father, Orestes, who ruled Italy in his name"), and so does the deep dive. The card, though, tells readers the boy held power on his father's behalf. Something like "a boy whose father ruled in his name" / "um menino em cujo nome o pai governava" would fix it. c3 is `supported`; the error is in the card passage.

**The trail does not have zero blockers: it has one.**

## Editorial notes

1. **`latin_to_portuguese` c3 (`partial`, both locales) and card block 2:** the Instituto Camões source hedges ("Pensa-se que por volta do ano 600 o latim vulgar não fosse falado…": it is thought that by around 600…). The claim and the card ("By around AD 600, Vulgar Latin had given way to new languages" / "Por volta do ano 600, o latim vulgar já tinha dado lugar…") state it as fact. The core is backed, and readers learn the same thing either way. A small hedge ("By around AD 600, it is thought…") would match the source.
2. **`roman_roads` quiz 3 misconception (both locales):** "roads like the Appian Way near Rome, which weren't the rule across the empire" / "que não eram a regra em todo o império" goes beyond c10. That source (Birmingham Roman Roads Project) speaks only of Britain. The explanation itself correctly says "In Britain, for example". The answer stays unambiguous.
3. **`gladiators_death` deep dive, "And the thumb?" (both locales):** "The thumbs-down idea took hold with Pollice Verso" / "ganhou força com o quadro" is a little stronger than c13's support. Encyclopaedia Romana says the painting "perpetuated that mistaken notion", and Phoenix Art Museum credits the modern reading mainly to the film. "Spread" (as c13 itself says) would be closer.
4. **`gladiators_death` card block 1:** "a rich diet" / "comiam bem" paraphrases BBC History's "high-energy diet". Next to the deep dive's "mostly vegetarian, heavy on grain", "rich" could be read as luxurious. "High-energy" would avoid the tension.
5. **Details that are in the cited snapshots but not in any excerpt:** `gladiators_death` deep dive "Lucius Raecius Felix, who had 12" (BBC History, same paragraph as the c8 excerpts); "researchers in Austria" (World Archaeology, Medical University of Vienna); `roman_roads` "an archaeological park" (AP: "The whole area is a protected archaeological park"); `roman_concrete` "a team led by geologist Marie Jackson" (BBC names her as lead author, and Smithsonian calls her a geologist). All are correct. Adding the phrases to the c8, c9, c13 and c9 excerpts would make the mechanical check cover them.
6. **Source record, not lesson text:** `src_web_archive_org_web_20080703070609_http_f7be2f.json` (Instituto Camões, cited by `when_rome_fell` c2 and `latin_to_portuguese` c3–c5) has `"language": "en"`, but the page is in Portuguese. I did not change it.
