## Editorial notes

1. **`eyeglasses_invention` pt-BR card block 1:** "É principalmente por causa desse sermão" (mainly because of this sermon) goes further than the en ("a key reason") and Medievalists.net ("based partly on this sermon"). Gale also cites an earlier reference from 1289. Use "em boa parte" or similar.
2. **`eyeglasses_invention` hook and quiz 2 (both locales):** the hook states as fact that the friar met the inventor, and quiz 2 gives the friar's silence as _the_ reason we don't know who invented glasses. Both match c2 and c3, but Medievalists.net is more cautious: glasses "seemed to spring up from many inventors' imaginations at the same time – making it impossible to pin down who the first inventor was, no matter what the good brother may have said". The card body gets it right ("he said … he had met"). Consider "says he met" in the hook and a quiz explanation that mentions the doubt. The answer is still unambiguous.
3. **`eyeglasses_invention` c13 and the title answer:** Britannica, which the lesson cites, says framed reading lenses were in use "both in Europe and China at this time, and it is a matter of controversy whether the West learned from the East or vice versa", and it attributes the introduction of eyeglasses in Italy to Alessandro di Spina of Florence. The lesson hedges c13 ("seem to be"), so this is not a blocker. A sentence on the China question would mark the dispute more fully.
4. **`eyeglasses_invention` c12 and deep dive (both locales):** "in the 1720s" simplifies Gale's "1725-1730". "Around 1725–1730" would be exact.
5. **`medieval_universities` coverage (summary, cardText, takeaway, both locales):** the lesson's central point, that the university was invented in medieval Europe, has no claim of its own. It follows from c2, c3 and c5, and the cited Catholic Encyclopedia says it directly: "it is generally held that the universities first arose in the Middle Ages". Consider adding that as a claim.
6. **`medieval_universities` hook (both locales):** "even the word 'university' comes from the Middle Ages" is loose. The Catholic Encyclopedia says universitas was a legal term that "got its meaning from the Corpus juris civilis" (Roman law). What is medieval is its use for a body of teachers and students. Something like "even the idea of a 'university'" would be safer.
7. **`medieval_universities` pt-BR card block 1:** "Rapazes como ele" (lads like him) clashes with the Catholic Encyclopedia: "These students were not boys, but mature men; many of them were beneficed clergymen." The en says "Students like him". Use "Estudantes como ele".
8. **`medieval_universities` en c11 claim text:** the comma before "José Bonifácio" makes a run-on sentence; the pt-BR uses a semicolon. Wording only.
9. **`mechanical_clocks` c4, card block 2 and quiz 2 (both locales):** the lesson gives only one reason why clocks counted equal hours (the foliot was hard to readjust daily). Gale gives two: the clock was "made partly as a sounding device and partly as an aid to astronomers", so it used the astronomers' equal hours, and because it was hard to readjust daily it never followed the seasonal hours. The deep dive's "the astronomers' twenty-four equal hours" points at this. The quiz answer is still right and its distractors are clearly wrong. Consider naming the astronomy purpose in the card or the quiz explanation.
10. **`arabic_numerals` coverage (card block 2 and quiz 1 misconception, both locales):** "which is why Europeans called the digits Arabic" answers the title, but no snapshot states the reason for the name. It is a fair inference from c2 (the numerals reached Europe from the Arab world), and it is the standard explanation. A source that says it outright would close the gap.
11. **`arabic_numerals` c6 and quiz 3 (both locales):** c6 quotes MacTutor's "introduced … into Europe", while c11 dates the first European document with these numerals to 976. The card's wording ("made them known to Italian merchants") avoids the clash, so only the claim text needs care. Quiz 3's explanation states as fact ("routine accounts were still kept in Roman numerals") what c9 presents as one study's argument. The deep dive's "One study argues" is the right level.
12. **`gutenberg_press` c4 (high-stakes) and deep dive:** "more than 150 copies" comes only from the Harry Ransom Center. Britannica, also cited for c4, says "The original number of copies of this work is unknown". The completion date has two publishers. Consider "an estimated 150 or more" or a second source for the number.
13. **`gutenberg_press` c14 (high-stakes):** the 13 May 1808 decree has three publishers. "Fleeing Napoleon's troops" and "the first printing equipment came with the royal fleet" rest on Congresso em Foco alone, a news site writing about the Imprensa Nacional's anniversary. Revista UFG, already cited for c13, links the move of the court to Napoleon and could be added for that part.
14. **`gutenberg_press` card block 2 (both locales):** "In Brazil, the story only began in 1808" is firmer than the sources. PublishNews mentions printing shops in Recife and Rio in 1706–1724 and machines seized in 1747, and Revista UFG speaks of "várias tentativas". The deep dive says this correctly ("there was no official press until 1808, even though there had been several attempts"). Something like "In Brazil, printing was only allowed from 1808" would match.
